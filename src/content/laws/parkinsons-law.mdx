<Section id="plain" title="In plain words">
<Analogy>
If your teacher gives you a week to write an essay, it takes a week. If they give you a day, it somehow takes a day.

The essay didn’t change. The time you were given did.
</Analogy>

Work stretches to fill the space we leave for it. In design, that means a long form *feels* like a big job, so people slow down, hesitate or leave. The fix is to make the job genuinely smaller.
</Section>

<Section id="why" title="Why it happens">
Historian Cyril Parkinson first wrote this as a joke about government offices, where staff kept growing even when the work didn’t. The idea stuck because everyone recognizes it: when there’s time, we fill it with second-guessing, extra steps and polishing.

For users, every field, step and decision is time. If a task *looks* long, people expect it to take long, and they spend that time or put it off for later. Designers can’t control people’s schedules, but they can **cut how much work a task needs**.

<div class="callout">
  <strong>Tip:</strong> showing how long something will take (“2 minutes, 3 steps”) helps too. It sets a small, clear time box, so the task doesn’t feel open-ended.
</div>
</Section>

<Section id="examples" title="Real-world examples">
<ul class="examples">
  <li><strong>Amazon 1-Click</strong><p>Your address and payment are already saved, so buying takes a single tap instead of a full checkout.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Browser autofill</strong><p>Chrome and Safari fill in names, addresses and cards, cutting form time from minutes to seconds.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Typeform</strong><p>It tells you up front how long a survey takes (“takes 2 minutes”), which makes people more likely to start and finish.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Apple Pay and Google Pay</strong><p>They replace a whole payment form with a quick confirmation.</p></li>
</ul>
</Section>

<Section id="apply" title="How to apply it">
<Checklist>
- **Prefill everything you already know**, like name, email, address and country.
- **Support autofill** with the correct input types and autocomplete attributes.
- **Remove optional fields**, or move them to later.
- **Tell people how long it will take**, such as “3 steps” or “about 2 minutes.”
- **Offer shortcuts** for repeat actions, like reordering, saved carts or one-tap payment.
</Checklist>
</Section>
