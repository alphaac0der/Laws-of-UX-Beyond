<Section id="plain" title="In plain words">
<Analogy>
Two restaurants serve the exact same pasta. One has clean tables and a well-designed menu. The other has sticky tables and a menu printed in Comic Sans.

Most people will say the first one’s pasta tastes better, and they’ll forgive it more if the food arrives a bit late.
</Analogy>

We judge with our eyes first. When something looks well made, we assume it **works** well too, and we’re more patient when it doesn’t.
</Section>

<Section id="why" title="Why it happens">
Before you’ve clicked anything, your brain has already formed a first impression in a fraction of a second. A neat, consistent, good-looking interface sends a signal: *someone cared about this*. That feeling spills over into how we judge everything else, including how easy it is to use.

This works both ways. A beautiful design makes people more tolerant of small problems, but it can also **hide** those problems during testing, because users are less likely to complain about something they like the look of.

<div class="callout">
  <strong>Common mistake:</strong> using visual polish to cover up a confusing flow. People may say they like it in a test, then still fail the task. Watch what users <em>do</em>, not just what they say.
</div>
</Section>

<Section id="examples" title="Real-world examples">
<ul class="examples">
  <li><strong>Apple product pages</strong><p>Calm layouts and beautiful photography make the products feel premium and easy to use before you’ve touched one.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Stripe</strong><p>Its polished website and docs signal that the payment product is trustworthy, which matters a lot when you’re handing over money.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Notion</strong><p>Clean typography and lots of whitespace make a very powerful tool feel approachable to beginners.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Banking apps</strong><p>Studies of online banking find that people trust better-looking sites more, even when the features are the same.</p></li>
</ul>
</Section>

<Section id="apply" title="How to apply it">
<Checklist>
- **Get the basics right first:** consistent spacing, alignment and a limited set of fonts and colors go a long way.
- **Use good looks to build trust** on high-stakes screens like checkout, sign-up and payment.
- **Don’t let polish hide problems.** In usability tests, measure task success, not just “do you like it?”
- **Keep it consistent.** One beautiful screen next to a messy one makes the whole product feel unreliable.
</Checklist>
</Section>
