<Section id="plain" title="In plain words">
<Analogy>
Think of a time you were so absorbed in a game, a drawing or a song that you looked up and two hours had vanished.

That’s flow: the task was hard enough to hold your attention, but not so hard that you wanted to quit.
</Analogy>

Flow is the state of **deep, effortless focus**. Products can’t force it, but they can make it much easier to get into, and much easier to break.
</Section>

<Section id="why" title="Why it happens">
Psychologist Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi studied artists, athletes and surgeons who described losing track of time while working. He found flow happens when a few conditions line up:

<ul class="examples">
  <li><strong>Balanced challenge</strong><p>The task stretches your skill a little, without overwhelming it. Too easy leads to boredom, too hard leads to anxiety.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Clear goals</strong><p>You know what you’re trying to do right now.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Immediate feedback</strong><p>You can see straight away whether what you did worked.</p></li>
  <li><strong>No interruptions</strong><p>Nothing pulls your attention away.</p></li>
</ul>

<div class="callout">
  <strong>Interruptions are flow’s enemy.</strong> A pop-up, a slow response or a confusing error can knock people out of flow instantly, and getting back in takes much longer than getting knocked out.
</div>
</Section>

<Section id="examples" title="Real-world examples">
<ul class="examples">
  <li><strong>Video games</strong><p>Good games adjust difficulty as you improve, keeping you in the flow channel for hours.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Duolingo</strong><p>Lessons adapt to your level, give instant right-or-wrong feedback, and are short enough to finish in one go.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Figma and code editors</strong><p>Keyboard shortcuts, instant previews and autosave let experts work without breaking focus.</p></li>
  <li><strong>iA Writer</strong><p>Its focus mode hides everything except the sentence you’re writing.</p></li>
</ul>
</Section>

<Section id="apply" title="How to apply it">
<Checklist>
- **Give instant feedback** for every action (see the Doherty Threshold).
- **Make goals clear** at each step, so people always know what to do next.
- **Adapt to skill:** simple paths for beginners and shortcuts for experts.
- **Protect focus.** Avoid pop-ups, interruptions and unnecessary confirmations during deep work.
- **Save automatically** so people never have to stop and worry about losing work.
</Checklist>
</Section>
