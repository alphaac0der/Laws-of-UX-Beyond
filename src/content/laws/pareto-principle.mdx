<Section id="plain" title="In plain words">
<Analogy>
Look in your wardrobe. You probably wear the same few favourite items most of the time, while the rest just hang there.

A small part of what you own does most of the work.
</Analogy>

In most products, **a handful of features get most of the use**, and a handful of problems cause most of the complaints. Knowing which ones tells you where your effort will matter most.
</Section>

<Section id="why" title="Why it happens">
Italian economist Vilfredo Pareto noticed that about 20% of the people in Italy owned about 80% of the land. Since then, people have found the same lopsided pattern almost everywhere: a few customers bring most of the revenue, and a few bugs cause most of the crashes.

The numbers aren’t always exactly 80/20. It might be 70/30 or 90/10. The point is that **results are rarely spread evenly**, so treating everything as equally important wastes effort.

<div class="callout">
  <strong>Don’t delete the other 80% blindly.</strong> A rarely used feature can still be essential for some people, like “reset password” or accessibility settings. Use the principle to set priorities and layout, not as an excuse to cut.
</div>
</Section>

<Section id="examples" title="Real-world examples">
<ul class="examples">
  <li><strong>Microsoft</strong><p>It found that fixing the top 20% of most-reported bugs would eliminate about 80% of crashes and errors.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Banking apps</strong><p>Most put “send money” and “check balance” on the home screen, because those few tasks make up most visits.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Microsoft Word’s ribbon</strong><p>The Home tab holds the handful of commands people use constantly, with the rest tucked into other tabs.</p></li>
  <li><strong>App store reviews</strong><p>Teams often find that a few recurring complaints account for most bad reviews, so fixing those lifts ratings fast.</p></li>
</ul>
</Section>

<Section id="apply" title="How to apply it">
<Checklist>
- **Use analytics** to find which features and pages get most of the use.
- **Give the vital few the best spots**, like the home screen, primary buttons and shortcuts.
- **Tackle problems by impact.** Fix the issues that affect the most people first.
- **Move rarely used features** a step back, into “More”, settings or search, instead of deleting them.
- **Revisit regularly.** The top 20% can change as your product and users change.
</Checklist>
</Section>
