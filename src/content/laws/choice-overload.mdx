<Section id="plain" title="In plain words">
<Analogy>
You open Netflix to watch something. Twenty minutes later you’re still scrolling, and you end up rewatching an old favourite, or nothing at all.

With so much to choose from, choosing became the hard part.
</Analogy>

More choice sounds like a good thing. But past a certain point, it makes deciding **harder, slower and less satisfying**, and many people simply walk away.
</Section>

<Section id="why" title="Why it happens">
In the famous “jam study,” a grocery store showed either 24 or 6 flavours of jam. The big display attracted more people, but shoppers were about **10 times more likely to buy** from the small one.

Every option has to be compared with the others, and the number of comparisons grows fast. On top of that, more options means more chances to regret: *“maybe a different one was better.”* People feel less sure about what they picked, or avoid deciding entirely.

<div class="callout">
  <strong>How is this different from Hick’s Law?</strong> Hick’s Law is about <em>time</em>: more options means slower decisions. Choice overload is about <em>giving up and regret</em>: too many options means people decide not to decide, or feel worse about their choice.
</div>
</Section>

<Section id="examples" title="Real-world examples">
<ul class="examples">
  <li><strong>Netflix</strong><p>Personalized rows like “Top picks for you” and the auto-playing preview cut thousands of titles down to a handful worth considering.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Spotify’s Discover Weekly</strong><p>Instead of searching 100 million songs, you get 30 picked for you each week.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Meal kits like HelloFresh</strong><p>They solve “what’s for dinner?” by offering a small weekly menu instead of every possible recipe.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Squarespace and Canva</strong><p>Their templates are grouped by goal (portfolio, shop, blog), so people only browse a relevant few.</p></li>
</ul>
</Section>

<Section id="apply" title="How to apply it">
<Checklist>
- **Curate.** Show a small set of good options first, and let people expand if they want.
- **Recommend.** Highlight a best pick, a popular choice, or what fits their stated needs.
- **Let people narrow down** with filters, categories and a few simple questions.
- **Make options easy to compare** with consistent details shown side by side.
- **Use smart defaults** so people can move on without deciding at all.
</Checklist>
</Section>
