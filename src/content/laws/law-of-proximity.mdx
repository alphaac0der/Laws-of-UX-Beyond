<Section id="plain" title="In plain words">
<Analogy>
At a party, you see three people laughing close together and one person standing a few steps away.

Without hearing a word, you know who’s in the conversation and who isn’t. Distance told you.
</Analogy>

Our eyes group things automatically. Before we read a single word, we’ve already decided **what goes with what** based on how close things are. In UI design, spacing tells people how things are related.
</Section>

<Section id="why" title="Why it happens">
The Gestalt psychologists found that the brain doesn’t see a screen as a pile of separate pieces. It looks for **patterns and groups**, because that’s faster than handling every item on its own. Being close together is one of the strongest grouping signals there is. It can be stronger than color or shape.

This makes spacing a very cheap design tool. You often don’t need borders, boxes or divider lines to show structure. **Whitespace can do the job.**

<div class="callout">
  <strong>Rule of thumb:</strong> the space <em>inside</em> a group should be clearly smaller than the space <em>between</em> groups. A label should sit closer to its own field than to the field above it.
</div>
</Section>

<Section id="examples" title="Real-world examples">
<ul class="examples">
  <li><strong>Google search results</strong><p>Each result’s title, link and snippet sit tightly together, with a bigger gap before the next result. There are no borders, only spacing.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Website footers</strong><p>Links are stacked in tight columns under headings like “Company” or “Support.” The gaps between columns show the categories at a glance.</p></li>
  <li><strong>iPhone Settings</strong><p>Related settings are grouped into blocks separated by space, so “Wi-Fi” and “Bluetooth” feel like one family and “Notifications” like another.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Chat apps</strong><p>Messages sent in a row by the same person are stacked close together. A bigger gap shows that someone else is now talking.</p></li>
</ul>
</Section>

<Section id="apply" title="How to apply it">
<Checklist>
- **Keep labels close to their inputs**, closer than they are to anything else.
- **Use a spacing scale** (e.g. 4, 8, 16, 24, 32px) with small steps inside groups and bigger steps between them.
- **Try whitespace before borders.** Add lines or boxes only if spacing alone isn’t enough.
- **Squint at your design.** If the blobs you see don’t match the real groups, adjust the spacing.
- **Keep it consistent.** The same kind of relationship should get the same amount of space everywhere.
</Checklist>
</Section>
