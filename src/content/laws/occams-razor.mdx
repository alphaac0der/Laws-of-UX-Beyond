<Section id="plain" title="In plain words">
<Analogy>
A Swiss Army knife with 30 tools is impressive. But when you just want to spread butter, a simple butter knife is faster, safer and easier.

More isn’t better if it doesn’t help with the job at hand.
</Analogy>

Every extra button, option and sentence is something people have to read, understand and decide to ignore. If it doesn’t help them do what they came to do, it’s **getting in the way**.
</Section>

<Section id="why" title="Why it happens">
William of Ockham was a 14th-century philosopher. His idea, often summed up as “don’t add more than you need”, was about explanations: the simplest one that fits the facts is usually the best.

In design, it means that among solutions that work, **the one with fewer parts usually wins**. It’s easier to learn, has fewer places for things to go wrong, and is cheaper to build and maintain.

<div class="callout">
  <strong>Simple is not the same as “less.”</strong> Removing something people actually need doesn’t make a design simpler, it makes it broken. The goal is the fewest parts that still <em>fully</em> do the job (see Tesler’s Law).
</div>
</Section>

<Section id="examples" title="Real-world examples">
<ul class="examples">
  <li><strong>Google’s homepage</strong><p>One search box. For over 25 years, it has resisted adding clutter to its most important page.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Apple’s product line</strong><p>When Steve Jobs returned in 1997, Apple cut dozens of products down to four, making it far easier to choose.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Dropbox</strong><p>It started with one simple idea, a folder that syncs everywhere, instead of a complicated set of file-management features.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Calm and Headspace</strong><p>Their home screens lead with one big “start” action instead of a menu of options.</p></li>
</ul>
</Section>

<Section id="apply" title="How to apply it">
<Checklist>
- **Start from the task**: what does the user need to finish it? Remove anything that doesn’t serve that.
- **Question every element.** If you removed it, would anyone notice or complain?
- **Prefer one clear way** to do something over several half-good ways.
- **Ask for information later**, only when it’s needed.
- **Test after cutting** to make sure the simpler version still works for real people.
</Checklist>
</Section>
