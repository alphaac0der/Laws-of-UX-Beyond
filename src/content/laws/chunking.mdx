<Section id="plain" title="In plain words">
<Analogy>
Try remembering F-B-I-C-I-A-N-A-S-A. Hard. Now try FBI, CIA, NASA. Easy.

Same ten letters. Three chunks instead of ten pieces.
</Analogy>

Our memory can only juggle a few things at once, but each “thing” can be a **group**. Chunking packs information into meaningful groups, so people can take in much more without feeling overloaded.
</Section>

<Section id="why" title="Why it happens">
George Miller noticed that the limit of short-term memory isn’t really about individual pieces, it’s about **chunks**. Experts seem to remember more because they’ve learned to see bigger chunks: a chess master sees a familiar position, not 20 separate pieces.

In interfaces, chunking happens through **grouping and structure**: spacing, headings, steps and formatting. A wall of text or a 16-digit number gives the brain nothing to hold onto. The same content in small groups is easy to scan and remember.

<div class="callout">
  <strong>Chunks should be meaningful.</strong> Splitting a form into random groups of 3 fields doesn’t help. Group by topic, like contact details, address and payment, so each chunk has a clear purpose.
</div>
</Section>

<Section id="examples" title="Real-world examples">
<ul class="examples">
  <li><strong>Phone and card numbers</strong><p>Almost everywhere, they’re shown in groups (555 123 4567, 4929 3817 2635 4015) to make them readable.</p></li>
  <li><strong>News and blog articles</strong><p>Headings, short paragraphs and bullet lists break long text into bites that are easy to scan.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Multi-step checkouts</strong><p>Shipping, delivery and payment are separate steps instead of one giant form.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Apple Music and Spotify</strong><p>Huge libraries are chunked into albums, playlists and genres, so you browse groups instead of thousands of songs.</p></li>
</ul>
</Section>

<Section id="apply" title="How to apply it">
<Checklist>
- **Format long numbers and codes in groups**, like phone numbers, cards, IBANs and license keys.
- **Break text up** with headings, short paragraphs and lists.
- **Group form fields by topic**, and split long forms into steps.
- **Use spacing and containers** to make chunks visible (see the Law of Proximity).
- **Keep chunks small:** three to five items per group is a good rule of thumb.
</Checklist>
</Section>
