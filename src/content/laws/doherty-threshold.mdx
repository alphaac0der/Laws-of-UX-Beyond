<Section id="plain" title="In plain words">
<Analogy>
Talking to a friend face to face feels natural. Talking on a video call with a one-second lag feels awkward. You interrupt each other and lose your train of thought.

Nothing about the conversation changed. Only the delay did.
</Analogy>

When a computer answers fast enough, it feels like it’s keeping up with you. When it’s slow, you stop *doing* and start *waiting*, and waiting breaks your focus.
</Section>

<Section id="why" title="Why it happens">
In the early 1980s, people believed a 2-second response time was fine. Two IBM researchers showed that when systems responded in **under 400 milliseconds**, people worked much faster, made fewer mistakes and stayed engaged. Each faster response encouraged a faster next step from the user.

Roughly, response times feel like this:

<ul class="examples">
  <li><strong>Under 100 ms</strong><p>Feels instant, as if you directly caused it.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Under 400 ms</strong><p>Feels responsive. You stay in the flow.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Around 1 second</strong><p>You notice the wait, but you keep your train of thought.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Several seconds or more</strong><p>Your attention drifts and you start doing something else.</p></li>
</ul>

<div class="callout">
  <strong>Can’t make it faster?</strong> Make it <em>feel</em> faster. Show a pressed state, a skeleton screen or a progress bar immediately. A quick acknowledgement is often enough to keep people engaged.
</div>
</Section>

<Section id="examples" title="Real-world examples">
<ul class="examples">
  <li><strong>Instagram likes</strong><p>The heart turns red the moment you tap it. The app updates the server in the background, a trick called an “optimistic update.”</p></li>
  <li><strong>YouTube and LinkedIn</strong><p>Grey skeleton boxes appear instantly while content loads, so the page feels like it’s already arriving.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Google Search</strong><p>Suggestions appear as you type, keeping you moving instead of waiting for a results page.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Figma</strong><p>Moving things on the canvas happens instantly on your screen, even while changes sync with collaborators.</p></li>
</ul>
</Section>

<Section id="apply" title="How to apply it">
<Checklist>
- **Aim for under 400 ms** between an action and a visible response.
- **Acknowledge every tap immediately** with a pressed state, even if the real work takes longer.
- **Use optimistic updates** for actions that almost always succeed, like likes, saves and toggles.
- **Show skeleton screens or progress** for anything that takes more than about a second.
- **Measure real-world speed** on slower phones and networks, not just on your fast laptop.
</Checklist>
</Section>
