<Section id="plain" title="In plain words">
<Analogy>
Look at the Olympic logo. You see five simple rings, not dozens of curved pieces weaving in and out of each other.

Your brain picks the simplest story that explains the picture.
</Analogy>

Prägnanz (German for roughly “conciseness” or “good form”) means our eyes **simplify** what we see. Faced with something complicated or unclear, we fill in gaps and settle on the simplest shape that fits.
</Section>

<Section id="why" title="Why it happens">
Our brains are always trying to save effort. Making sense of a messy, complex image takes work, so the visual system jumps to the simplest interpretation, such as circles, squares and straight lines, and moves on. That’s why we see shapes in clouds and complete a logo that’s only partly drawn.

For designers, this cuts both ways. People will **understand simple forms instantly**, but if a design is cluttered they may simplify it **into the wrong meaning**.

<div class="callout">
  <strong>In practice:</strong> icons, logos and layouts built from simple, regular shapes are recognized faster and remembered better than detailed, irregular ones.
</div>
</Section>

<Section id="examples" title="Real-world examples">
<ul class="examples">
  <li><strong>The Olympic rings</strong><p>We see five overlapping circles, the simplest reading of a complex interlocking shape.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Apple’s logo</strong><p>A simple apple silhouette with one bite. Instantly recognizable, even as a tiny icon.</p></li>
  <li><strong>System icons</strong><p>iOS and Material icons use simple geometric outlines so they’re readable at 16 pixels.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Loading spinners</strong><p>A few dots or a partial circle are enough for us to see a complete, spinning ring.</p></li>
</ul>
</Section>

<Section id="apply" title="How to apply it">
<Checklist>
- **Build icons and illustrations from simple shapes** like circles, squares and clean lines.
- **Reduce visual noise.** Remove decoration that doesn’t carry meaning.
- **Use clear, regular layouts** like grids and aligned edges, so the structure is easy to see.
- **Test at small sizes and at a glance**, to check that people see what you intended.
- **Avoid ambiguous shapes** that could be simplified into the wrong thing.
</Checklist>
</Section>
