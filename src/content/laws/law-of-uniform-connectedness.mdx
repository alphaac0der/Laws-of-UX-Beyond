<Section id="plain" title="In plain words">
<Analogy>
On a subway map, stations far apart on the page are clearly on the same line because a colored line runs through them.

The line tells you more than the distances do.
</Analogy>

When elements are **physically connected**, by a line, an arrow or a shared bar, we see them as one unit. This is even stronger than being close together or looking alike.
</Section>

<Section id="why" title="Why it happens">
Stephen Palmer and Irvin Rock argued that connectedness is one of the most basic ways our brain organizes what we see. A connected region is processed as a single object first, and then the brain looks at the details inside it.

That’s why a line between two items can beat proximity: in the demo above, dots that sit close together suddenly stop looking like pairs as soon as different dots are joined.

<div class="callout">
  <strong>Great for steps and flows.</strong> Connecting lines make the order of steps, the path of a process, or the link between related items obvious without extra words.
</div>
</Section>

<Section id="examples" title="Real-world examples">
<ul class="examples">
  <li><strong>Delivery trackers</strong><p>Amazon and food delivery apps connect each stage with a line, so you see the whole journey and where your order is.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Subway maps</strong><p>Colored lines connect stations into routes, regardless of their actual distance.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Figma prototypes</strong><p>Arrows connect screens, making the flow between them obvious at a glance.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Chat threads</strong><p>Slack and Reddit draw thin lines to connect replies to the message they’re answering.</p></li>
</ul>
</Section>

<Section id="apply" title="How to apply it">
<Checklist>
- **Connect sequential steps** in checkouts, onboarding and trackers with a line or bar.
- **Use lines or arrows** to show relationships in diagrams, threads and flows.
- **Fill the connection to show progress**, such as completed steps in a solid line and future steps faded.
- **Keep connectors subtle** so they guide the eye without becoming the main thing.
- **Don’t connect unrelated items.** A line is such a strong signal that it will create relationships people trust.
</Checklist>
</Section>
