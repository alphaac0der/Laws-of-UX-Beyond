<Section id="plain" title="In plain words">
<Analogy>
You had a long, tiring holiday with one amazing sunset dinner, and the flight home was smooth and early.

Months later, you remember it as a great trip. The boring bits and small annoyances have faded.
</Analogy>

We don’t remember experiences as an average of every moment. We remember **the peak** (best or worst) and **the end**. Those two moments shape whether people come back.
</Section>

<Section id="why" title="Why it happens">
In a famous study, people held their hand in painfully cold water twice: once for 60 seconds, and once for 90 seconds where the last 30 seconds were slightly less cold. Most chose to repeat the **longer** trial. More pain overall, but a better ending.

Our memory saves effort by keeping a few snapshots instead of the whole film. The most intense moment and the final moment become the summary.

<div class="callout">
  <strong>Negative peaks count too.</strong> One frustrating moment, like an error that loses your work, can become the peak that defines the whole experience. Find those moments and fix them first.
</div>
</Section>

<Section id="examples" title="Real-world examples">
<ul class="examples">
  <li><strong>Mailchimp</strong><p>After you send a campaign, a cartoon hand gives you a high five. A tiny delightful moment at the end of a stressful task.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Uber</strong><p>Rides end with a smooth arrival, a clear receipt and a rating. Even if the ride was average, the ending feels tidy.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Disney theme parks</strong><p>They design for memorable peaks like the castle and the fireworks, so long queues fade from memory.</p></li>

<Section id="apply" title="How to apply it">
<Checklist>
- **Map the journey** and find its peaks, both the best and worst moments, and its end.
- **Fix the worst moments first**, like confusing errors, lost data or surprise costs.
- **Make endings feel finished:** clear confirmation, what happens next, and a thank-you.
- **Add small moments of delight** at key successes, without slowing people down.
- **End loading and progress on a fast note.** A finish that speeds up feels quicker overall.
</Checklist>
</Section>
