<Section id="plain" title="In plain words">
<Analogy>
At a food court, each restaurant has its own counter. You never wonder whether the fries belong to the pizza place or the burger place.

The counters draw invisible lines around each group.
</Analogy>

When elements share a **background, border or container**, we read them as one group, even if they’re spaced the same as everything else.
</Section>

<Section id="why" title="Why it happens">
Psychologist Stephen Palmer showed that a shared region is such a strong grouping cue that it can **override closeness** (proximity) and even **similarity**. Two dots far apart inside the same box look more related than two close dots in different boxes.

That’s why cards are so common in interfaces: a card instantly says “everything in here is about the same thing.”

<div class="callout">
  <strong>Common mistake:</strong> boxes inside boxes inside boxes. When every element has its own border, nothing stands out and the screen looks busy. Use regions for real groups, and let spacing handle the small stuff.
</div>
</Section>

<Section id="examples" title="Real-world examples">
<ul class="examples">
  <li><strong>Pinterest and Airbnb</strong><p>Every listing or pin is a card, so the photo, title and price clearly belong to one item.</p></li>
  <li><strong>iOS Settings</strong><p>Related settings are grouped on rounded white panels against a grey background.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Twitter/X quote posts</strong><p>The quoted post sits inside a bordered box, making it clear it’s separate from the reply around it.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Forms</strong><p>Checkout pages often put “Shipping” and “Payment” in separate panels, so each set of fields reads as one step.</p></li>
</ul>
</Section>

<Section id="apply" title="How to apply it">
<Checklist>
- **Use cards or panels** to group content about one thing, like a product, post or plan.
- **Prefer a background tint over a border** for a lighter, cleaner look.
- **Group form fields** that belong to the same step or topic.
- **Avoid nesting regions** more than one or two levels deep.
- **Combine with spacing:** clear gaps between regions make the grouping even stronger.
</Checklist>
</Section>
