<Section id="plain" title="In plain words">
<Analogy>
In a class photo where everyone wears school uniform, the one kid in a bright red jumper is the face you’ll remember.

Being different is what makes something memorable.
</Analogy>

When most things look alike, the **odd one out** grabs attention and sticks in memory. Designers use this to point people to the most important action.
</Section>

<Section id="why" title="Why it happens">
German psychologist Hedwig von Restorff found that when people studied a list of similar items with one item that was different, like a number among syllables, they remembered the different one much better.

Our brains are wired to notice change and difference, because historically, the unusual thing was often the important thing. In interfaces, contrast in **color, size, shape or motion** triggers the same response.

<div class="callout">
  <strong>It only works if you use it sparingly.</strong> If everything is highlighted, nothing stands out. And don’t rely on color alone: about 1 in 12 men has some form of color blindness, so add a size, shape or label difference too.
</div>
</Section>

<Section id="examples" title="Real-world examples">
<ul class="examples">
  <li><strong>Pricing tables</strong><p>The recommended plan is highlighted with a different color or a “Most popular” badge, so it’s the one people notice.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Notification badges</strong><p>A small red dot on an app icon stands out against everything else on your home screen.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Gmail’s Compose button</strong><p>It’s the one large, colored button in an otherwise calm sidebar.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Search results</strong><p>Your search words are shown in bold inside each result, so you can scan for matches.</p></li>
</ul>
</Section>

<Section id="apply" title="How to apply it">
<Checklist>
- **Pick one primary action per screen** and make it visually distinct.
- **Use contrast in several ways**, such as size, weight, shape and color, not color alone.
- **Keep everything else calm** so the highlight has something to stand out against.
- **Use motion sparingly.** It’s the strongest attention-grabber, and the most distracting.
- **Don’t make unimportant things stand out**, like ads or upsells, or people learn to ignore highlights.
</Checklist>
</Section>
