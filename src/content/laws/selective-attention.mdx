<Section id="plain" title="In plain words">
<Analogy>
At a noisy party you can follow one conversation and ignore all the others. But if someone across the room says your name, you hear it instantly.

Your brain filters the world, letting through only what seems relevant.
</Analogy>

We can’t pay attention to everything, so we **filter**. People on a website notice what matches their goal and skip the rest, sometimes including things that were right in front of them.
</Section>

<Section id="why" title="Why it happens">
In the 1950s, Colin Cherry showed that people listening to two voices at once could follow one and remember almost nothing of the other. In a famous later study, about half of viewers counting basketball passes didn’t notice a person in a gorilla suit walking through the scene.

Online, this filtering shows up as **banner blindness**: people have learned that boxes that look like ads are usually useless, so they skip anything that resembles one, even when it contains exactly what they need.

<div class="callout">
  <strong>Change blindness is related.</strong> If something on the screen changes while people are focused elsewhere, like a total updating or an error appearing far away, they often don’t notice. Show feedback right where the person is looking.
</div>
</Section>

<Section id="examples" title="Real-world examples">
<ul class="examples">
  <li><strong>Banner blindness</strong><p>Eye-tracking studies by Nielsen Norman Group show people rarely look at anything that resembles an ad, even useful content.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Inline form errors</strong><p>Good forms show errors next to the field with the problem. A message at the top of the page is easy to miss.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Google Maps navigation</strong><p>Only the next turn is shown big and clear. Everything else is reduced while you drive.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Focus modes</strong><p>iOS Focus and writing apps like iA Writer hide everything except the task at hand.</p></li>
</ul>
</Section>

<Section id="apply" title="How to apply it">
<Checklist>
- **Place information where people are looking** for their task, not in sidebars or banners.
- **Never style important content like ads**: avoid bright banners, flashing and “sponsored-looking” boxes.
- **Show feedback near the action**, like errors by the field and confirmation by the button.
- **Remove distractions** on focused tasks such as checkout, sign-up and writing.
- **Test with eye-tracking or observation** to see what people actually notice.
</Checklist>
</Section>
